## 1. The measure

`tools/rounds.js` now prints the buys of each side, for the recorded matches and for the bots by the same code:

```sh
node tools/strategies.js                                # once: it reads the demos into its cache
node tools/rounds.js --kinds fatal --seeds 1,2,3,4,5,6  # six maps; set A. Set B: seeds 7 to 12
```

- A full buy: four players of the side or more carry a rifle (AK-47, M4A1, AUG, SG 552), a sniper rifle that is
  no Scout, or the M249. Pistols: one primary at most. A force buy: all else.

  round with both sides on pistols after a round with a gun starts a half and counts for the first row only.
- The distance of a side to the players: the mean over those four rows of half the summed differences of the
  three shares.

## 2. The causes

1. **No change of a lesser gun.** A bot that held any primary bought no gun. One that lived with a FAMAS kept it,
   whatever its money.
2. **Grenades bought and never thrown.** A bot throws a grenade only at a lineup of the map, and the lineups are
   an option (spec 03, section 7). Plain fatalbot bought four grenades in each round with a rifle (1000 of its
   money) and threw 0.00 grenades a minute.
3. **Each bot bought alone.** A bot that was short of the money took a lesser rifle with an even chance. So a
   team was mixed in most rounds, and it never saved together.

## 3. The keep rule, written before the first run of a change

- Numbers of a rule are chosen on set A (seeds 1 to 6) only. A change stays when, on set B (seeds 7 to 12):
  1. the distance of the CTs' rows falls, and the distance of the Ts' rows does not rise by more than 3 points;
  2. the Ts' share by match does not rise by more than the error of the difference (gate G2);
  3. fatalbot against the reference bots does not fall by more than 5 points over both sets (gate G1, 384
     rounds). A gain there is no strength of play: the reference bots buy as before.
- Gate G4 is not judged by this work.
- The rows of the recorded matches must repeat to the round: a demo round has no buy.

- **The call of a team** (`buy.js teamCall`). The first bot of a team that buys makes the call of the round from
  what the bots of the team have, as players say it before a round. A buy: all of them but one at most hold a
  gun to keep or have the money of a full buy. Else a force buy after a round that the team won, and with a
  chance of 0.4 after one that it lost. Else a save.
- In a buy or a force buy each bot takes the best that its money gives: the rifle, the lesser rifle, a
  submachine gun (each with a vest), or a vest and a better pistol.
- In a save a bot spends only when it holds a gun to keep, or has the money of two buys.
- A teammate that no brain drives counts for nothing: a bot alone among people buys by its own money.
- **Commit `dd69a84` alone failed the rule**: with a buy or a save and nothing between, a team had a force buy in
  0 to 4% of its rounds, and the Ts' distance rose by 10.6 points on set B. The recorded teams force a buy in 16
  to 23% of their rounds, and they do not save after a round that they won (2% of those rounds). So the call got
  its third answer in `4be84f5`.
- **The frame has a new optional field**, `round.score` ({T, CT}): what the scoreboard shows to every player. The
  brain reads from it which side won the last round. Spec 02 names its source on a real server (the `TeamScore`
  message).
- **The chance of a force buy** was chosen on set A among 0.25, 0.4 and 0.55. The CTs' distance: 15.7, 12.0 and
  13.8 points. The Ts' distance: 17.9, 11.8 and 7.6 points.

## 6. What is still not the players' buy

- **The CTs after a won round** have a full buy in 56% of the rounds (the players' 77%). A CT that died in a won
  round has the reward and little more, which is short of the M4A1 with armor. Players hand a gun to such a
  teammate. The bots do not, and they pick no gun up from the floor.
- **The Ts are richer than the players' Ts**: a full buy in 72% of the rounds (64%). They buy no grenades. With
  the lineups of a map (`fatal-nades`) they buy them again.
- **After one lost round** the bots force a buy less often than the players (8% and 19% of those rounds; the
  players' 20% and 24%). A poor team of bots that forces has pistols and vests, which counts as pistols here.
