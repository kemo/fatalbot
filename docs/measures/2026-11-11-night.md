## 1. How the night was measured

Every number below comes from a clean copy of one commit (`git archive <commit>`), never from the work tree. One
measure set is these commands, with the seeds of set A or of set B:

```sh
# Gate G1: fatalbot against the reference bots. Set A: seeds 1,2. Set B: seeds 3,4.
node tools/arena.js --a fatal --b ref --maps de_dust2,de_inferno,de_train,de_aztec --rounds 12 --seeds 1,2 --sides both
# Gates G2 to G4: fatalbot against itself. Set A: seeds 1 to 6. Set B: seeds 7 to 12.
node tools/arena.js --a fatal --b fatal --maps de_dust2,de_inferno,de_nuke,de_train,de_aztec,de_cbble \
  --rounds 12 --seeds 1,2,3,4,5,6 --sides once
# The recorded matches, for de_dust2, de_inferno, de_nuke and de_train. Set A: seed 1. Set B: seed 2 (other demos).
node tools/demo-arena.js --map <map> --count 6 --rounds 10 --seed 1
node tools/demo-arena.js --map <map> --count 6 --rounds 10 --seed 1 --standins fatal
```

Set A at `47adee4` gives the numbers of the earlier documents again, to the round: the runs are deterministic.

**How much a number says.** The rounds of a match are not 12 samples. The money of a lost round makes the next
round harder, so a match goes 11:1 or 3:9. Blocks of 72 rounds of one build on one map (six matches, other seeds)
gave 41, 50, 49, 51, 59 and 47 rounds to the Ts: a spread of about 6 rounds. A change that draws one more random
number plays other rounds from then on, so a block of 72 rounds can differ by 15 rounds with no cause. A
difference on one map counts here only when it repeats on seeds that the change was not chosen on.

## 2. The bots that take over for a player: stuck (gate G3)

The takeover test of docs/measures/2026-11-10-replay-takeover.md showed bots that were stuck 6.2% of the time on
de_train and 2.5% on de_nuke. The guess of that document (a player stands where the graph has no floor) was
wrong. A log of every stuck spell, with its place, its goal and the seconds since the takeover, gave three other
causes:

| Cause | Where | Stuck seconds of 120 rounds |
|-------|-------|------|
| At the top of a ladder beside its floor the bot turned its view to the floor. The game moves a player on a ladder along the view, so the turn sent it down again. It swung between two heights. | de_train, one ladder | 607 of 703 |
| The floors of a platform have no way in the graph but one, at a ladder's top. The bot walked to that floor, then searched its way from the floor nearest to it, which has no way. It shook in one place. | de_nuke, the platform over the upper site | 243 of 377 |
| A climb holds no stuck clock. A ladder that the bot cannot take (under a hatch that only a ducked player fits) was never marked as failed: after each give-up the bot found the same way. | de_nuke, the hatch of the roof | 76 of 377 |

The changes, one commit each:

- `49c6db7`: at a ladder's top with the floor on the open side of the ladder's face, the bot keeps its face to
  the ladder, climbs until its feet clear the floor, then steps off sideways. A floor straight behind it takes a
  jump off the ladder.
- `97c8874`: a bot on an island of the graph starts its way from the joined floor that it stands at. Of the joined
  floors it takes the first that a straight walk reaches (the nearest can lie behind a wall). A bot on an island
  counts as able to reach the level, so it gets a goal there.
- `59c88e9`: a give-up marks the step of the way that the bot stood before, as a stuck walk does.

| Measure (the takeover bots, 480 rounds of set A) | `47adee4` | `59c88e9` |
|---------|------|------|
| Stuck, share of the time | 2.3% | 0.4% |
| Stuck seconds on de_train, 120 rounds | 703 | 59 |
| Stuck seconds on de_nuke, 120 rounds | 377 | 177, then the hatch case less |
| The side of the takeover bots wins, of 480 | 224 | 227 |
| fatalbot against the reference bots, 192 rounds | 69.3% | 75.5% |

What this says:

- **The fights are the players' fights.** Who dies first, and what a first death and the numbers at a plant are
  worth, agree with the recorded matches to a few points.
- **The pace is not.** The Ts of `fatal` run to a site at once: the first T stands in it at second 22, the
  players' at second 34. With the mined plans the pace is the players' (36 s), and as few CTs stand in the site
  when the Ts come (0.33, the players 0.41).
- **The bots plant too often** (68 to 71% of the rounds, the players 44%). The players' CTs stop the Ts before
  any T stands in a site in 31% of the rounds; the bots' CTs in 22 to 23%. Gate G4 (plants near the human
  matches) is not met. This is the next work on the team play.
- **With the plans the CTs lose more** (the Ts win 57%). The plans stay an option.

## 4. Gate G2: the CT side

Against itself the Ts of fatalbot won 58.6% of 432 rounds (the gate: 39 to 54%). The cause was not the fights
(section 3). By map, the Ts won 44 to 55% on five maps and 74% on de_aztec (864 rounds, seeds 1 to 12, commit
`59c88e9`).

The runs of both teams from their spawns, by the graph, at 240 units a second:

| Site | The first T at a way in | The CTs at their posts |
|------|------|------|
| de_aztec, site 0 | 14.8 s | 13.8 to 17.9 s |
| de_nuke, site 0 (the upper one) | 13.9 s | 14.7 to 17.8 s |
| every other site of the six maps | 12.6 to 23.1 s | 2.5 to 22 s earlier than the Ts |

On two sites the Ts are at the way in before the CTs are at their posts. The logs of de_aztec showed it: three
CTs shot within two seconds, on their run into the site.

The change (`af4314d`): each post says whether a defender is there in time, which is half a second before the
first attacker within 1000 units has a line to it. A CT that runs from its spawn takes a post in time. A site
with fewer than five such posts gets floors further back on the CTs' side, which see the site or a way in. A
rotation and a bot that takes over choose as before.

The change, in two forms:

2. `d8af7be` (kept): the same bot jumps by chance at the rate of its profile, and never over its count. No jump

## 6. Gate G4: the plant and the pace of the Ts

Section 3 showed two gaps: the bots plant in far more rounds than the players, and their Ts are in a site 12 s
before the players' Ts. Two more numbers of the recorded matches (10,843 rounds of four maps) beside the bots:

| Measure | The recorded matches | fatalbot at `d8af7be` |
|---------|------|------|
| Of the rounds with a T in a site: a plant | 63% | 92% |
| Of those rounds: no plant, the Ts win | 14% | 0% |
| Of those rounds: no plant, the CTs win | 24% | 8% |
| Plants with one or two CTs alive | 58% | 33% |
| The plant after the first T in the site, the middle | 7 s | 5 s |

The players' Ts fight for the site first and plant once it is theirs. The bots' carrier planted the moment it
stood in the site.

**The plant** (`90d76e6`): the carrier in the site plants once no enemy was seen or heard within 1000 units of
it for 3 s, by itself or by its team. Alone, or with under 40 s of the round left, it plants at once. Until then
it fights for the site with its team.

**The pace** (`a7858e1`): without a mined plan, each T waits on a way to the site, short of the way in, where no
post of the site sees it. The second of the first T in the site is drawn with a middle of 34 s (the players':
18, 22, 34, 54 and 72 s at p10, p25, p50, p75 and p90). Each T leaves its place within 2 s of the call.

| The first death is a CT | 48% | 47% | 42% | 55% | 51% |
| Rounds with a plant | 44% | 74% | 63% | 65% | 66% |

| The second of the first T in a site, the middle | 34 s | 22 s | 22 s | 34 s | 34 s |

The table above is from the seeds on which the lag was chosen. The rule for the next 24 seeds (13 to 36),
written before they were played: the lag stays in this record as a cause only if the Ts' share with it is lower
than without it by more than the error of the difference. Gate G2 counts as met only if the share of the kept
build over all 36 seeds is 54% or under; else spec 01 says that G2 is open, and the pace is the owner's decision.

The same three builds on those 24 seeds, and over all 36 seeds. The Ts' share is the mean over the matches of
12 rounds, with the error of that mean (a match spreads by about 22 points):

| Build | Seeds 1 to 12, 72 matches | Seeds 13 to 36, 144 matches | All 36 seeds, 216 matches |
|-------|------|------|------|

By the rule:

- The lag is a cause: on the new seeds the Ts win 4.3 points less with it (the error of the difference is 2.4),
  and 4.1 points less over all seeds (error 2.0).
- **Gate G2 is open with the pace.** Over all 36 seeds the Ts win 54.3% (the gate is 39 to 54%). Without the
  pace they win 52.1%. The pace costs the CTs about 2 points (error 2.1), and it gives the round the players'
  pace. The owner decides: `git revert a7858e1` takes the pace away and leaves the plant.

- The pace of a round is now the players' pace.
- Ts that all leave their places on one tick come round a corner together, and the CT at the post dies first in
  55% of the rounds. A team of players does not move on one tick: with up to 2 s between its players the first
  death is a CT in 51% of the rounds, and the Ts win 53%.
- The plants fell from 74% to 66% of the rounds. The players' 44% is still far: their CTs stop the Ts before any
  T stands in a site in 31% of the rounds.

## 7. A fault of the data: recordings that go on into another map

One recording of the second seed set gave a bot that was stuck 97% of the time. Its players stood inside walls:
the recording is named de_nuke and goes on into de_dust2. A scan of all 514 recordings found 105 of 14,744 rounds
in which half of the players or more start inside a wall of the named level: 28 of the rounds named de_nuke, 21
of the 129 named de_aztec, 34 of de_train (one round each in 25 recordings), 13 of de_inferno. The demo arena and
`tools/strategies.js` now leave such a round out (`9aed11e`), and docs/strategies.md is made again. The tables
of sections 3 and 4 were made before this and hold those rounds; on de_aztec they are 21 of 97 recorded rounds.
`tools/players.js` (the aim of the players) leaves them out from its next run; the tables of
docs/measures/2026-11-10-players-aim.md hold them (0.7% of the rounds).
