- **The base of the night** is commit `362beb4` (the commits after it to `2f16a96` change documents only). Every

## 1. What the players do after the first death (a measure, no change)

Three agents measured the recorded matches beside the bots; they changed no code. For the time from the first
death of a round to the plant, on de_dust2, de_inferno, de_nuke and de_train (10,814 recorded rounds; 288 rounds
of the bots, seeds 7 to 12):

| Measure | Recorded CTs | Bot CTs | Recorded Ts | Bot Ts |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| A step of 2 s in which the player stands | 17% | 36% | 10% | 20% |
| Windows of 10 s with 200 units or more between start and end | 72% | 23% | 84% | 68% |
| Windows of 10 s with over 600 units between start and end | 40% | 10% | 52% | 41% |
| Stands, in the windows under 200 units | 44% | 42% | 34% | 40% |

- **The recorded players travel after the first death.** A player that stays about one place stands as often as a
  bot does (44% against 42% for the CTs). The gap is that the bot CTs stay: they hold a post in 89% of that time.
- **Where the travel goes** (windows over 600 units): toward the site where the attack is in 52% of the CTs'
  windows, toward the place of a teammate's death in 51% of the windows that have one.
- **The bot Ts** are still at the waiting place of their plan in 35% of the steps after the first death.
- **Close to an enemy** (under 500 units) a recorded CT stands in 19% of the steps and a bot CT in 57%. Nothing
  was changed for that: steps at every moment of a wait cost 19 rounds on 2026-11-11.

Now the first T that the team sees at a site (or the killer of a teammate there) brings the defenders of the other
site over: the nearest first, one a second, and one of them stays. They go back when no T was seen at that site
for 20 s, or when the team sees an attack at another site. The whole team still rotates at 60%.

## 8. When the players jump and duck (a measure)

An agent counted jumps with one detector for the recorded players (64 matches of 20 samples a second or more,
1,804 rounds) and the bots (288 rounds at `4e98424`).

- **The jump count of the arenas is wrong for a recorded player.** `hosts/csbih/arena.js` reads a jump as the
  first tick off the floor with a rise over 200 units a second. For a bot that is a true take-off. For a recorded
  player on the rails of csbih-player the rise comes from a curve through its samples: a stand-up from a duck, a
  double duck and a step of a stair read as jumps, and most real jumps stay under 200. Of the 4.4 to 8.0 "jumps" a
  minute of the rails, 0.8 to 1.5 are real, and the gauge sees 28 to 48% of the real ones. The row "jumps a
  minute" of the arenas does not compare the bots with the players.
