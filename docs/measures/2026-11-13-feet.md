# 2026-11-13: the feet of a player: the tap of the duck, the real jumps, the chains of hops, the steps of a fight

The owner asked whether a bot takes the movement style of its player: "movement style / strafing / bunny hoping /
ducking (players in 1.6 often bind duck to mousescroll down and jump to mousescroll up so they can easily bunny hop
and move a bit quieter by ducking while running)".

The answer was: only in part. A bot had its player's jumps a minute and the part of its fights fought on the move
and ducked. It had no tap of the duck, and the count of the jumps was wrong.

## 1. What a tap of the duck is

The duck key down for one tick starts a duck. The game stands the player up from it 18 units higher
(`pm_shared` `PM_UnDuck`: on the ground the hull of a stand goes back 18 higher), and the player falls back in
0.2 s. The run keeps its speed, and the feet make no step sound in the air. A player with the duck on the mouse
wheel does this on a run. The core of csbih-player has the same rule (`pmove.js`): a duck of 1 to 10 ticks on a run
of 250 units a second gives a lift of 18 units, 20 ticks in the air and a run of 250 after it.

## 2. The measure

`hosts/csbih/arena.js gauge` now sorts each time in the air by its lift over the floor of its start:

- a lift of 10 to 27 units, from a stand, that lands within 6 units of its start in 0.5 s, is a tap of the duck;
- a lift of more than 27 units is a jump (a jump lifts 45);
- a flat jump whose top comes within 0.9 s of the top of the flat jump before is a jump of a chain;
- in a fight, the gauge counts the seconds with a step across the view and the changes of its side.

Before this, a jump was a rise of 200 units a second at the take-off. In a recording that is wrong three ways:

- A tap of the duck rises 18 units between two frames: counted as a jump.
- A stand out of a full duck does the same: counted as a jump.
- A jump whose start falls between two frames rises more slowly over the frame: not counted.

`test/style.test.js` holds the measure against a body of the game that taps from a stand, taps on a run, ducks and
stands twice and jumps twice: 2 taps and 2 jumps, for the body itself, and for its recording at 17 and at 33 frames
a second.

csbih-player has the same sorting for one player of a demo (`profile.js feetOf`, with its own test). On four demos
of de_dust2 the two agree: 1.55 and 1.71 taps a minute, 3.3 and 2.9 jumps, 27.6% and 24.3% of the flat jumps in a
chain, 0.90 s and 0.98 s of side steps for each change.

by `profile.js feetOf`. "Two halves" holds the number of one half of a player's demos against the other half
(Spearman, the players with enough in both halves):

| Measure | p10 | p50 | p90 | Two halves |
|---------|-----|-----|-----|------------|
| Taps of the duck a minute, out of a fight | 0.08 | 1.32 | 4.47 | 0.81 (913 players) |
| Jumps a minute | 1.39 | 2.90 | 5.03 | 0.56 (913) |

- The tap of the duck is a habit of the player: the same player taps as often in other demos.
- The old count of a profile was mostly taps, and it counted the freeze time too. The middle player had 9 "jumps" a
  minute and 2.9 real ones. A bot keeps its count of jumps to the profile's, so with these profiles every bot
  jumped wherever its way allowed a jump, whatever its player did.
- The taps come alone or a few in a row (six demos, 1,072 taps): 74% of the rolls are one tap, 14% two, 6% three.
  A tap has one more within 0.8 s in 34% of the cases.

Open: for the 169 players who have ten minutes in demos under 12 frames a second and in demos of 16 and over, the
taps a minute are 2.03 in the slow demos and 1.57 in the fast ones (the middle of each). The taps of the slow
demos have the right lift (60% at 18 units, the rest caught lower in their fall). The cause is not found.

## 5. The changes

1. `duckTap` (`moves.js`): a bot taps the duck on a calm run or wait, one tick each, at `duckTaps` a minute of its
   profile, in rolls (one more tap with a chance of 0.34, five at most). Never within 3 s of a sign of an enemy, in
   a hop, on a walk, on a ladder, or without room over its head and along its way.
2. `jumpRate` of a profile is the real jumps a minute of the live play (csbih-player `profile.js`).
3. `hopChain` of a profile is the part of the flat jumps in a chain. A bot with a chance of 0.5 at a place that
   allows a chain had 8.5% of its flat jumps in one, so the chance there is the profile's part times 5.9 (0.95 at
   most).
4. `strafeBeat` of a profile sets how long a step of a fight lasts; 0.9 s is the timing of before.
5. The default profile has the middle of the players: `jumpRate` 2.9 (was 6), `hopChain` 0.17 (was a chance of
   0.5), `duckTaps` 1.3, `strafeBeat` 0.9.
6. A bot behind its count of jumps takes almost the first place that allows a jump (`HOP_EAGER` 10, `HOP_KEEN` 40).

## 6. The bots after

The same 24 rounds, commit `a88f60c`:

| Kind | Jumps a minute | Taps a minute (run, stand, fight) | Flat jumps in a chain |
|------|----------------|-----------------------------------|-----------------------|
| fatal | 2.3 | 1.62 (0.91, 0.53, 0.17) | 9.0% of 78 |

- The taps out of a fight are 1.44 a minute against 1.3 of the profile, and 1.32 of the middle player.
- The jumps are inside the spread of the players (1.39 to 5.03), under the middle.
- The chains did not move: the places that allow a chain cap them at about 9% of the flat jumps, with any chance.
  The players have 20% to 28%. A bot of a player with few chains chains less; no bot chains as often as its player.
  This needs wider rules for the place of a hop (`HOP_STRAIGHT`, the view on the way), with the G3 gate watched.
